Chapter 1 · Introduction
1.1

Background of the Study

The rapid advancement of information and communication technology has fundamentally transformed the economic landscape of societies worldwide, reshaping how goods and services are produced, promoted, and exchanged. Among the most consequential developments is electronic commerce (e-commerce), which has evolved from a supplementary sales channel into a primary driver of economic activity across industries and geographic boundaries. Digital platforms have democratized access to markets, enabling producers of all scales to reach consumers far beyond traditional brick-and-mortar establishments (Volkova et al., 2021). Governments, NGOs, and private enterprises increasingly recognize e-commerce as a strategic tool for inclusive economic development, particularly in emerging economies where physical market infrastructure remains uneven (Hughes, 2025). In the Philippine context, smartphone proliferation, affordable mobile data, and government digital transformation initiatives have accelerated online commerce adoption, creating unprecedented opportunities for previously marginalized sectors (Rodrigo et al., 2024).

Despite these advancements, the benefits of digital commerce have not been equitably distributed. Persons with Disabilities (PWDs) represent one of the most persistently underserved populations in both physical and digital economic ecosystems. In the Philippines, PSA estimates millions of Filipinos living with some form of disability. While legislation such as Republic Act No. 7277 (Magna Carta for Disabled Persons) enshrines PWD rights to employment, livelihood, and economic participation, translation into practical, accessible opportunities remains deeply inadequate (Mendoza, 2023). PWD entrepreneurs face compounding barriers: limited business information access, absence of structured digital platforms tailored to their needs, insufficient institutional support, and low digital literacy (Malhotra et al., 2025; Mendoza, 2023).

In Region IX, the Area Vocational Rehabilitation Center (AVRC) — a DSWD-operated institution in Mampang, Zamboanga City — serves more than sixty-six (66) PWD clients trained in handweaving, handicrafts, decorative items, recycled products, and other artisanal crafts. While training programs succeed in developing productive skills, the absence of a structured, accessible digital sales channel limits economic impact: clients rely on episodic physical fairs, informal social media postings by staff, and word-of-mouth referrals. This disconnect between skills development and market access is a critical institutional gap that technology can address.

A dedicated web-based smart e-commerce platform provides a concrete, sustainable solution: a permanent, accessible, organized marketplace promoting products to consumers across Region IX and beyond, designed with accessibility at its core — TalkBack voice accessibility support, high-contrast design, simplified navigation, WCAG alignment (W3C, 2018; Sundaramoorthy et al., 2024) — plus robust monitoring, management, and reporting tools for LGU staff and AVRC administrators, operationalizing national disability policy and UN SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities).

1.2

Project Context

Inclusive Market is conceptualized within AVRC Region IX's operational environment and the broader policy landscape of disability-inclusive economic development. AVRC operates under DSWD Region IX as the primary institutional home for PWD rehabilitation and livelihood training in Zamboanga Peninsula; its 66+ active clients produce handmade crafts, handwoven products, decorative items, recycled goods, souvenir items, and gift articles — yet market exposure remains sporadic and income inconsistent without a digital channel. The project sits at the intersection of three domains: web-based e-commerce, inclusive/accessible interface design, and government-supervised livelihood support. It enables PWD clients to sell through an organized storefront while AVRC personnel and LGU staff monitor listings, manage accounts, oversee inventory, and track transactions through an administrative panel. The project aligns with NCDA's mandate, RA 7277 guarantees, the DSWD community-based rehabilitation framework, and Region IX's local development agenda — a replicable model for digital market access within a government rehabilitation institution.

1.3

Purpose and Description

The primary purpose is to design and develop a fully functional, accessible, and secure web-based smart e-commerce platform bridging PWD livelihood production at AVRC Region IX and actual product commercialization. "Smart" denotes: a smart search engine (fuzzy matching, search highlighting, recent searches, trending products); smart recommendations (composite scoring with price-range, seller matching, diversity scoring); smart alerts (severity-level monitoring across 7 conditions); smart catalog filtering (price range, rating, category filters); and dynamic Trending/New Arrivals sections. Accessibility is prioritized via TalkBack voice support, text-to-speech, voice commands, and reading mode with word highlighting under WCAG standards — high contrast, enlarged targets, simplified navigation, clear cues.

Three user groups are served: PWD clients as registered sellers/managers (product CRUD, order management, analytics, payout requests, PWD ID verification); AVRC/LGU staff as administrators (monitoring, approvals, payment provider configuration); and consumers/buyers who purchase via dynamically configured payment methods (PayMongo, Stripe, PayPal, Maya, GCash). Authentication is implemented through Supabase Auth with role-based access control, backed by PostgreSQL with Row-Level Security — upgraded from the original localStorage-only prototype. Built with React 19, Vite, and Tailwind CSS 4 as an install-free web platform.

1.4

Objectives

1.4.1 General Objective

To design and develop Inclusive Market — a fully accessible, web-based smart e-commerce platform enabling 66+ PWD clients of AVRC Region IX to promote and sell livelihood products online, with comprehensive administrative tools for product management, inventory monitoring, order processing, payment configuration, and transaction oversight — advancing economic empowerment and livelihood sustainability of PWDs in Region IX.

1.4.2 Specific Objectives (SO-01 … SO-13)

SO-01 · Smart Platform

Design and develop a smart web-based e-commerce platform serving as centralized digital marketplace for handmade crafts, handwoven products, decorative items, recycled products, souvenirs, gift articles.

SO-02 · Accessibility Suite

TalkBack voice support, TTS, voice commands, reading mode with word highlighting, inclusive interface design per WCAG so PWD users navigate independently.

SO-03 · Product Management

Comprehensive module for adding/editing/categorizing listings with images, descriptions, pricing, availability — accurate, organized, visually appealing catalog.

SO-04 · Inventory & Orders

Stock tracking, order processing, status management, transaction records — real-time operational visibility for admins and staff.

SO-05 · Multi-Provider Payments

PayMongo, Stripe, PayPal, Maya, GCash with admin enable/disable toggles and API key management — secure, locally accessible processing compliant with RA 10173.

SO-06 · Per-User Persistence

Independent carts and wishlists per registered user, scoped storage ensuring isolation across sessions (now Supabase-backed rows keyed by auth user id).

SO-07 · Admin Dashboard

LGU/AVRC control panel: user management, seller/product approval workflows, payment provider config, sales monitoring, reports, transaction oversight.

SO-08 · Seller Payouts

Payout request submission, admin approval workflow, history tracking, CSV export — earnings through configured payout methods.

SO-09 · Smart Search

Fuzzy matching across names/sellers/descriptions/categories, result highlighting, recent searches, trending display, keyboard navigation.

SO-10 · Smart Recommendations

Composite scoring: category similarity, price proximity, seller match, ratings, diversity — "You Might Also Like" on detail pages.

SO-11 · Catalog Filters

Price range (min/max + slider), minimum rating, active filter badges with individual removal, Most Popular & Newest sorts.

SO-12 · Dynamic Homepage

"Trending Now" scored by rating × reviews; "New Arrivals" sorted by date added — discovery beyond static featured products.

SO-13 · Evaluation

Structured usability/accessibility testing with PWD clients and AVRC personnel using validated instruments (ISO 25010, SUS, Likert) within the project timeline.

1.6

Scope and Limitations

1.6.1 Scope

  • Registration/authentication with RBAC (Supabase Auth sessions)
  • Catalog management: categories, images, pricing, stock tracking
  • Browsing/search with autocomplete, category filter, sorts
  • Per-user cart & wishlist persisted server-side per account
  • Multi-step checkout with validation; provider selection from enabled set
  • PayMongo · Stripe · PayPal · Maya · GCash admin toggles
  • Order placement, status timeline, reviews & ratings
  • Smart search, recommendations, filters, trending/new arrivals
  • Alert engine (7 conditions × severity)
  • Seller dashboard, CRUD, order mgmt, analytics charts, payouts + CSV, PWD ID upload
  • Admin dashboard, users, categories, approvals, orders, transactions, reports, activity logs, payment & platform settings

1.6.2 Limitations

  • Only AVRC Region IX PWD-produced products; no other branches/regions
  • Internet connectivity required; no offline mode
  • No native Android/iOS app — responsive browser access only
  • Image upload depends on configured storage bucket quotas
  • Live payment capture requires valid merchant API keys per provider
  • Evaluation limited to specified period & selected AVRC respondents; not large-scale commercial deployment
  • No WebSocket/push real-time features

Smart Catalog Filtering

Price range min/max + slider, rating floors (3+/4+/4.5+), removable badges, Most Popular (reviews×rating) & Newest sorts.

Stripe

Global card payment platform; integrated configurable provider.

TalkBack Voice Support

Screen-reader/voice navigation compatibility enabling audio-guided operation for visual/motor impairments.

Text-to-Speech (TTS)

Web Speech API speech synthesis with adjustable rate & voice selection.

Voice Commands

SpeechRecognition hands-free actions: "search", "go to cart", "go home", "scroll down", "stop listening".

Usability

Effectiveness, efficiency, satisfaction in specified context (ISO 9241-11); evaluated via structured testing.

Wishlist

Heart-toggle saved-products list persisted per user.

WCAG

Browser-accessed application requiring no installation — React 19, Vite, Tailwind CSS 4.

3.1.5 Design of Software System Products & Process

Presentation Layer

React 19 + Vite + Tailwind CSS 4 responsive UI for all three roles; WCAG 2.1 AA via semantic HTML, ARIA labels, contrast ratios, keyboard navigability; TalkBack compatibility through ARIA live regions, alt text, focus management; TTS (Web Speech API), SpeechRecognition commands, reading mode, flash alerts.

Application Logic Layer

Seven context providers — Auth, DataStore, Cart, Settings, Voice, Toast, VisualAlert — each owning one domain. Service modules: paymongoService, stripeService, paypalService, mayaService, gcashService, paymentProviders (unified config), gmailService, googleAuth. Credentials read from secured config at runtime.

Data Layer (Upgraded)

Now: Supabase PostgreSQL — 24+ tables under im_ schema prefix, Row-Level Security policies per role, atomic checkout RPC, server-persisted preferences. Legacy fallback: localStorage keys (im_ namespace) seed mock data and persist device prefs.

Provider Hierarchy (outermost → innermost)

AuthProvider → DataStoreProvider → CartProvider (user-scoped) → SettingsProvider → VoiceProvider → ToastProvider → VisualAlertProvider

3.2

Research Instruments

ISO/IEC 25010 Evaluation Instrument

Software quality questionnaire covering Functional Suitability, Performance Efficiency, Compatibility, Usability, Reliability, Security, Maintainability, Portability — administered to IT-expert evaluators; 5-point Likert items aggregated per characteristic.

System Usability Scale (SUS)

Standard 10-item instrument (5-point Likert) administered after task-based sessions; yields 0–100 score. Acceptability benchmark: ≥ 70 (NFR-01), ≥ 80 = "Excellent" band.

Task-Based Usability Protocol

Scripted tasks mirroring SO-02/SO-09: locate a product via voice command, complete search-with-highlight, add to cart, checkout flow, adjust font size/high contrast. Metrics: completion rate, time-on-task, error count, independent-completion flag.

Respondent Matrix

  • • PWD clients (sellers) — accessibility & seller flows
  • • AVRC personnel — admin oversight tasks
  • • LGU staff — reports/settings tasks
  • • General buyers — shopping journey
  • • IT experts — ISO 25010 evaluation
3.3

Statistical Tools

Weighted Mean (x̄) = Σ(f × x) ⁄ Nf = frequency per scale point · x = scale weight (1–5) · N = total respondents
ScaleVerbal InterpretationMean Range
5Strongly Agree / Very Satisfactory4.21 – 5.00

SUS scoring: odd items −1, even items −(5−x), sum × 2.5 → 0–100. ISO 25010 characteristic scores = weighted mean of its items, interpreted via the same Likert ranges. Decision rule: system passes when overall weighted mean ≥ 3.41 AND SUS ≥ 70 AND all critical tasks independently completable by ≥ 80% of PWD participants.

Ethical Considerations

Data Privacy (RA 10173)

Personal data collected minimally (name, email, role, PWD ID reference); processed only for platform operation; RLS restricts access to owners and authorized admins; no third-party sale of data.

Informed Consent

Evaluation participants receive plain-language consent forms explaining purpose, procedures, voluntary withdrawal rights — obtained before any testing session.

Vulnerable Participants

PWD participants supported with accessible consent materials (large print, TTS-read), companion allowance, flexible scheduling at AVRC premises.

Credential Security

Passwords hashed by Supabase Auth; API keys stored in protected configuration; no secrets committed to source; plaintext never rendered in DOM (NFR-04).

Academic Integrity

All reviewed works properly cited (APA); AI-assisted tooling disclosed per university policy; findings reported honestly including limitations.

Institutional Clearance

Data gathering coordinated through AVRC Region IX management and DSWD IX protocols; adviser-endorsed instruments.

Chapter 5 · Conclusion and Recommendations
5.1

Conclusion

Inclusive Market successfully demonstrates that a dedicated, institutionally embedded, accessibility-first e-commerce platform can bridge the documented gap between PWD livelihood production at AVRC Region IX and real digital-market participation. All thirteen specific objectives were carried through analysis, agile delivery across seven sprints, and implementation — culminating in a Supabase-backed, Vercel-deployed system with role-gated buyer, seller, and administrator experiences, atomic inventory-safe checkout, five configurable Philippine-relevant payment providers, a full WCAG-aligned assistive suite, and intelligent discovery features. The system stands apart from commercial marketplaces precisely where those marketplaces fall short for this community: PWDs are positioned as empowered sellers, not merely consumers, under AVRC/LGU co-management.
